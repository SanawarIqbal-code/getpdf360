How to convert a scanned PDF to Word

A scanned PDF is just a picture of a page, so normal converters can't edit it. Here's how to turn it into a Word document you can edit, for free, using OCR.

By Sanawar Iqbal · Updated 27 November 2026

Why normal PDF to Word doesn't work on scans

A PDF made on a computer contains real text. A scanned PDF contains only an image of the page, so a normal converter has no text to work with. OCR (optical character recognition) reads the letters in the image and turns them into real, editable text.

Quick test: try to select a word in your PDF. If you can't, it's a scan and you need OCR.

Convert it with OCR

  1. Open OCR PDF.
  2. Choose the document's language: English, Urdu, Hindi or Arabic.
  3. Choose Word as the result.
  4. Select your scanned PDF, or a JPG or PNG photo of the page.
  5. Download the .docx file. OCR takes a few seconds per page.

Tips for the best results

  • Clear, straight scans work best. Blurry or slanted pages give more mistakes.
  • Printed text works much better than handwriting. Handwritten notes are hard for any OCR tool.
  • Pick the right language. Reading an Urdu page as English gives nonsense.
  • Expect some tidying. Tables, columns and fancy layouts may need fixing in Word.

Other options

  • Want the PDF to look the same but be searchable? Choose Searchable PDF instead of Word.
  • Only need the words? Choose Plain text.
  • Long document? OCR works on up to 20 pages at a time. Use Split PDF to do it in parts.
